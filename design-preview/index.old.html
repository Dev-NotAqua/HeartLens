I

The quiet panel

Four decisions,
one printed page.

The overlay stays at the edge of the screen and answers the next question. Nothing opens, nothing covers the board, nothing plays for you.

A plan you can commit to.

Seven comps that fit the lobby, ordered by placement and what you already own. Each row shows its core cards, owned pieces and the turn to commit.

Up to seven comps · owned core · commit turn

Pick for the lobby you got.

Hearthlens reads the tribes in play before the first buy and adjusts each hero's average place. The best pick gets a marigold mark; the rest stay visible so you can disagree.

Lobby-adjusted placement · pick rate · sample size

Buy the board you have.

Every minion is scored against your board, your comp's core cards and the gold in hand. Triples, core cards and freezes are called out before you spend.

Score · reason · freeze advice

Close calls stay close.

Discover, Dark Gift and trinket options are ranked. When the numbers are within noise, Hearthlens says so instead of inventing a winner.

Best pick · close call · no fake certainty

II

One language, three surfaces

The page, the desk,
the match.

The same ink, paper and letterpress marks follow you from the almanac into the dashboard and onto the board. The overlay is a note laid on the game, not another game window.

III

The pool

One pool,
two sources.

A seeded baseline gives the advisor something useful on day one. Games players choose to share are merged by sample size. Nothing is replaced: the mix shifts as the pool grows.
